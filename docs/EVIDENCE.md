## The engine in brief

Rules are in [RULES.md](RULES.md); the code follows it. Shares are integers, money is integer micro-dollars, ratios are exact rationals (BigInt); there is no floating point. Every rounding remainder is reported, never silently dropped.

- **Post-money SAFE, cap only:** each SAFE gets exactly its purchase amount divided by its cap, of the capitalization just before the round. With a discount, it converts at the lower of the cap price and the discounted round price, solved by exact fixed-point iteration (fails loudly after 1,000 iterations).
- **Priced round:** the pool top-up sits in the pre-money, so it dilutes existing holders and SAFEs but not the new investor. Price = (pre-money − target × post-money) / (issued + SAFE shares), rounded down to 4 decimals.
- **Exit waterfall:** non-participating preferred takes its preference or converts, whichever pays more; ranks pay in order, pro rata within a rank; options exercise only above their strike.
- **Golden vectors** (`vectors/gv-1.json` to `vectors/gv-7.json`; GV-3 and GV-4 live in `test/`): GV-1 gives a $2.00 price, 15,000,000 fully diluted shares, Fund 4,500,000, SAFE 1 at $0.50 and SAFE 2 at $1.00. GV-2 at a $20M exit: Ada $6,111,111.11, Bayo $3,361,111.11, SAFE 1 $1,222,222.22, SAFE 2 $305,555.56, Fund $9,000,000.00.


## How NEAR is used

- **Sputnik DAO** (testnet factory `sputnik-v2.testnet`) is the board and the treasury. Approvals are ordinary FunctionCall proposals; the contract sees the DAO account as the caller. In step 10 the DAO also spends its own treasury through a FunctionCall proposal on the token (`ft_transfer_call` with the 1 yoctoNEAR a NEP-141 transfer needs). A 2-of-3 council needed two votes in the spike and the demo.
- **NEP-141** for payments: a self-deployed test USDC with a public faucet (no testnet USDC was used).
- **Named accounts** for every role, **NEP-297-style contract events**, and **plan hashes** (sha256 of RFC 8785 canonical JSON) that the board approves and the verifier recomputes.
- NEAR AI Cloud and NEAR Intents are **not** used. Cross-chain payment is described, not run, because NEAR Intents has no testnet.

## Demo video

**Demo video:** [`demo/rounds-demo.mp4`](demo/rounds-demo.mp4) (about 3.5 minutes, narrated in plain language; the confidential part was re-recorded on 2026-11-13, the rest is the 2026-11-11 cut). It opens with a 15 second fly-over of the live page (made by `scripts/intro-film`), then shows the **confidential mode** on its testnet sandbox (`rsbx-zkrounds-097917.testnet`) with the **wallet-derived keys** ([docs/WALLET-KEYS.md](docs/WALLET-KEYS.md)): the board member publishes an encryption key derived from their wallet; the private cap table is built and proved in the browser with the holder's key derived from the wallet, and the claim and the board's table are sealed on chain; the board member's wallet opens the table and it re-seals to the on-chain root; a table with one number changed is refused; a private payout is opened with a $5 relayer fee; then a holder with no files at all recovers the claim and key from the wallet and claims to a brand-new account through a relayer, receiving the share minus the fee. Then the transparent mode, unchanged from the earlier cut: the round modeler, the guided round, a shared payout, the in-browser hash re-check, and the board proposals card, held still on screen while each is explained. Waiting for the chain is cut out, and every silent gap of more than about half a second is squeezed to a 0.4 second pause by `scripts/compress-gaps.mjs` (the page is sped up through the gap, speech is never touched), so the narration flows. The parts were recorded separately on different testnet sandboxes by `scripts/record-demo.mjs` and joined with ffmpeg, so account names differ between parts. The test signer (a throwaway key, including for the wallet's message signature) stands in for a wallet, and the claim goes through a relayer run locally (the live site has none, so there the holder's wallet signs the claim). The transparent parts predate the role bar and the draft and sign-off features, so they show the older page layout. The token-sale (SAFT) scene of the earlier cut is not in this one; it is covered by `make verify-tokens`.
