NEW HERE? WHAT YOU ARE LOOKING AT

A cap table is the list of who owns how much of a startup: the founders, early investors, employees with stock options, and anyone who invests later.

For a group that funds something together (a crypto startup, an investment club, a contributor collective), that table is usually still a private spreadsheet, and the group's vote cannot be tied to the exact numbers it approved. Rounds works out the numbers exactly, holds the pooled money in escrow, has the group's DAO approve each round on the NEAR blockchain (the shares and the money paid for them, in one fingerprint), and lets anyone re-check it. It is a testnet prototype and models equity, SAFTs and a project-token reserve with vesting grants.

The company here is a made-up example. You can change a funding round and watch everyone's share change. Then, on a test network with fake money, you can run a round yourself, and the page checks it against the blockchain.

Quickest way to see it work: go to Run a round on testnet and press the highlighted button each time (about two minutes), or press Verify at the bottom to re-check a finished round.

Plain-English glossary
Cap table
The table of who owns how many shares of a company.
Funding round / Series A
A moment when new investors put money in and receive new shares. "Series A" is a company's first big priced round.
Valuation and share price
What the company is said to be worth before the round, divided by its shares, gives the price of one share.
SAFE
An early investment note: an investor pays now and gets shares later, at the next priced round, usually at a discount or capped valuation.
Option pool
Shares set aside to give to employees later. Making it bigger leaves less for the existing owners.
Common and preferred shares
Founders hold common. Investors usually hold preferred, which gets paid back first if the company is sold.
Exit
When the company is sold. The exit simulator shows who gets how much of the sale price.
Plan hash
A fingerprint of a round's exact numbers: the shares issued, who pays how much, and the deadline. If anything changes, the fingerprint changes, so an approval of one plan cannot be reused for another.
Board / DAO
The group that approves a round on the blockchain. Here it approves the fingerprint, not a vague "yes".
Testnet
A practice version of the blockchain where the money has no value.
